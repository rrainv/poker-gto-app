Getting started and Playbook
Build a spot, inspect its recommendation, then explore the current view.

Getting started

Choose a workspace from the rail: Playbook to build or progress a spot, Training for generated decisions, and Equity to calculate win chances. Select cards from a card slot; unavailable cards are already in use in that workspace.

Common terms: Unopened means no voluntary raise has occurred. Facing a raise, 3-bet, or 4-bet describes the action you must respond to. SPR is the effective stack divided by the pot.

Study a decision

Choose or build a spot, set cards where needed, and inspect the primary recommendation and Strategy Mix. Open “What goes into this decision?” for the supporting facts, then use Current spot views to switch between Decision, Range Matrix, and Range Category Comparison. The table can be collapsed when you want more room for analysis.

Facing context: Use the action context to describe what happened before your turn. Facing size is the current wager context, not necessarily the exact amount you must call. When a call amount is known, Riverline labels that price separately.

Range Matrix and comparison

Preflop Matrix cells represent hand classes. Their tint shows the dominant action and the stacked band shows the full strategy mix. Hover or focus a cell for a quick frequency cue; select it for action-frequency detail in the inspector.

A full postflop Matrix expansion is not available yet. Use Decision for an exact-hand postflop recommendation. Range Category Comparison instead compares fixed heuristic preflop samples by the categories they make on a board; it is descriptive, not a weighted range-versus-range calculation.

Scenario and Hand

Scenario mode: Use this for an arbitrary study snapshot. You supply the context, so it does not represent a complete legal hand history and some exact pricing or history facts can be unknown.

Hand mode: Use this to progress a legal hand. Actions, history, and prices are derived from the hand as it develops.

Accounting: Home has no Riverline rake or deduction. In ClubGG-style games, each seated player contributes 0.1 bb once per hand outside the contestable pot.

Training and tools
Practice legal decisions, calculate equity, and tune presentation preferences.

Training

Training generates legal hand trajectories. Choose a decision family and Assistance level, then answer using a visible action. Study hints are optional coaching prompts. After your answer, review the verdict, your chosen action, the current reference frequencies, and the shared analysis.

Use session progress to track the current run. Replay and current-drill controls let you revisit the same generated trajectory.

Equity

Add known hands or leave opponents random, then set board and dead cards as needed. Auto uses exact enumeration when practical and otherwise uses Monte Carlo simulation. Preparation can be indeterminate; during a simulation, progress, rate, and estimated time remaining can appear.

Results identify whether they are exact or simulated and show Equity, Win, and Tie for each player. Simulation is an estimate. Cancel stops the calculation and keeps your configuration intact.

Settings and presentation

Settings includes the current theme choices, language, audio, and card presentation. Choose Card Style from Classic Mirrored, Tournament, Clean Corner, or Clarity Corner. Rank Style lets you display ten as T or 10; it changes presentation only.

Strategy source and limits

Riverline currently uses a deterministic heuristic strategy source for decision recommendations and Training reference frequencies. Exact-hand postflop recommendations are heuristic, and the preflop Matrix reflects the same source.

Use Riverline to structure study and compare its recommendations with your own review. Its recommendations and Training grades are not proof of an optimal poker strategy.
